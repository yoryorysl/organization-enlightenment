What Is the Illuminati?

The term "Illuminati" has been used to describe different groups and ideas throughout history. The most historically documented organization associated with the name was the Bavarian Illuminati, founded in 1776 by Johann Adam Weishaupt in Ingolstadt, Bavaria.

Modern discussions frequently use the name to describe theories or narratives about a continuing secret or global organization. Such modern claims are different from the documented history of the eighteenth-century Bavarian group.

This website presents information about these ideas while encouraging visitors to distinguish historical evidence from modern claims and speculation.

Global Unity

Global unity refers to the idea that people from different cultures, countries and backgrounds can cooperate around shared principles such as peace, education, personal development and service to others.

The philosophy presented on this website places emphasis on learning, responsible conduct, mutual respect and constructive participation in society.

Historical Origins

A brief overview of the historically documented Bavarian Illuminati.

1748

Birth of Johann Adam Weishaupt

Johann Adam Weishaupt was born in Ingolstadt, Bavaria. He later became a professor of canon law.

1776

Foundation

Weishaupt founded the historical Illuminati in Ingolstadt on May 1, 1776. The organization was associated with Enlightenment-era ideas concerning reason, education and social reform.

1780s

Suppression

Bavarian authorities prohibited the organization and it declined during the 1780s.

Historical distinction: The documented Bavarian Illuminati of the eighteenth century should not automatically be treated as proof of modern claims about a continuing worldwide organization.
